## Training Philosophy & Methodology

### The 70-20-10 Rule

| Component | Percentage | Activities |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Hands-On Practice | 70% | Exercises, applications, projects |
| Group Discussion | 20% | Debates, peer review, Q&A |
| Lecture/Theory | 10% | Core concepts, framework introductions |

### Golden Rules for Facilitators

1. **Never lecture for more than 15 minutes** without a hands-on activity
2. **Demo first, then let them try** - show it live, then let participants replicate
3. **Celebrate mistakes** - failed prompts are the best teaching moments
4. **Use real APC scenarios** whenever possible - relevance drives engagement
5. **Energy check every 45 minutes** - if energy drops, use an energizer immediately
6. **Bilingual delivery** - present in Arabic, keep technical terms in English
7. **No participant left behind** - pair fast learners with slower ones

## Facilitation Techniques

### Opening Each Unit

1. Start with a **hook** (surprising statistic, provocative question, or quick demo)
2. State **learning objectives** clearly: "By the end, you will be able to..."
3. Connect to **their daily work**: "How many of you have spent hours doing X?"
4. Set **expectations**: "We'll do Y exercises, and by the end you'll have Z"

### Managing Hands-On Exercises

| Phase | Trainer Action | Duration |
|-------|---------------|----------|
| Setup | Explain the exercise clearly, show expected output | 2-3 min |
| Work | Circulate the room, help stuck participants | Exercise time |
| Share | 2-3 participants share their results | 3-5 min |
| Debrief | Highlight best practices, common mistakes | 2-3 min |

### Handling Common Situations

**"The AI gave me a wrong answer"**
- Perfect! This is a teaching moment about hallucinations and verification
- Ask: "How would you verify this?" and "What would you change in your prompt?"

**"I can't access the tool"**
- Have backup screenshots of expected outputs
- Pair the participant with someone who has access
- Switch to an alternative tool (e.g., if ChatGPT is down, use Claude)

**"This doesn't apply to my job"**
- Ask them to describe their specific challenge
- Work together to craft a prompt for their actual use case
- This often becomes the most powerful learning moment

**"I'm already advanced"**
- Assign them as a "table coach" to help others
- Give them the Extension Tasks from the worksheets
- Ask them to try the same exercise with a different AI tool and compare

**"Wi-Fi is down"**
- Switch to discussion-based activities (Framework Selection Challenge, Prompt Debugging)
- Use printed backup materials
- This is why having offline worksheets printed is essential

### When to Deploy Energizers

| Signal | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Multiple people checking phones | Immediate energizer (2-3 min physical) |
| Questions have stopped | Switch to group activity |
| Post-lunch drowsiness (1:00-1:30 PM) | Start with a standing energizer |
| Long exercise fatigue | Break the exercise into smaller chunks |
| End-of-day decline (3:30+ PM) | Use a competition or team challenge |

## Group Formation Strategy

### Day 1: Mixed Departments

- Form groups with diverse departments (1 HR + 1 Production + 1 Maintenance + 1 IT)
- Encourages cross-pollination of ideas
- Use for Units 1-2

### Day 3: Same Department

- Regroup by department for Unit 4 (Industrial Prompts)
- Allows deep dive into department-specific challenges
- Each group works on their own real problems

### Day 5: Self-Selected Teams

- Let participants choose their capstone teams
- Maximum 3 people per team
- Encourage at least one "technical" person per team

## Assessment & Scoring Guide

### Daily Quick Checks

At the end of each day, collect the Daily Feedback Form (3 questions):
1. What was the most useful thing you learned today? (1 sentence)
2. What do you still find confusing? (1 sentence)
3. Energy level: 1-5 stars

Review feedback each evening and adjust next day's pace accordingly.

### Unit Quizzes

- Administer at the end of each unit (or start of next day as review)
- 10 questions, 10-15 minutes
- Use as formative assessment (learning tool, not punishment)
- Review answers as a group - the discussion IS the learning

### Capstone Scoring

| Criterion | Weight | What to Look For |
|----------|--------|-----------------|
| Prompt Quality (30%) | CO-STAR or other framework used, clear constraints, iterative improvement shown |
| Tool Mastery (25%) | Used 3+ tools appropriately, knew when to use which |
| Practical Value (25%) | Solves a real problem, could be used tomorrow at work |
| Security Awareness (10%) | Mentioned data privacy, used enterprise-safe practices |
| Presentation (10%) | Clear, concise, showed process not just results |

## Troubleshooting Guide

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Tool is down/unavailable | Switch to alternative tool, use screenshots |
| Participant has no laptop | Pair with neighbor, provide printed materials |
| AI output is in wrong language | Add "Respond in [language]" to prompt |
| Exercise takes longer than planned | Cut to 2 groups sharing instead of all |
| Advanced participant is bored | Assign as table coach, give extension tasks |
| Participant is frustrated | One-on-one coaching during break, simplify the task |
| Projector fails | Use TV/monitor if available, or gather around one laptop |
| Content too fast | Skip extension exercises, focus on core ones |
| Content too slow | Add spontaneous challenges, let advanced students demo |

## Bilingual Delivery Tips

### Arabic-First Approach

- Deliver explanations in Arabic
- Keep all technical terms in English (Token, Prompt, Context Window, Hallucination, Temperature, RAG)
- Write prompts in English (better AI performance), explain in Arabic
- When participants write prompts in Arabic, discuss the "linguistic tax" impact

### Code-Switching Best Practices

- Introduce English term first, then explain in Arabic
- Use the English term consistently throughout (don't alternate translations)
- Create a **terminology wall** (flip chart with English terms and Arabic explanations)
- Encourage participants to use English terms in their prompt library

## Pre-Training Preparation Checklist (1 Week Before)

- [ ] Confirm room booking and AV equipment
- [ ] Test Wi-Fi speed with 20+ simultaneous connections
- [ ] Verify all participant accounts are created and working
- [ ] Print all worksheets (one packet per participant per day)
- [ ] Prepare USB drives with backup materials (slides, sample data)
- [ ] Test all AI tools from the training room (some corporate firewalls block them)
- [ ] Prepare name tags and table numbers
- [ ] Stock refreshments for breaks
- [ ] Send Welcome Kit email to all participants
- [ ] Prepare certificates with participant names
- [ ] Create a WhatsApp/Teams group for the cohort
- [ ] Load sample data files on a shared drive accessible to all

## Post-Training Follow-Up

### Week 1 After Training

- Send all materials (slides, worksheets, prompt library) via email
- Share the NotebookLM training notebook link
- Post a "Challenge of the Week" in the cohort group

### Month 1 After Training

- Send a follow-up survey: "Which tools have you used? What barriers did you face?"
- Offer a 1-hour virtual Q&A session
- Highlight success stories from participants

### Quarter 1 After Training

- Measure adoption metrics (how many using AI tools regularly?)
- Collect ROI stories (time saved, problems solved)
- Plan advanced training for top performers
