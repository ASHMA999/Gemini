# Unit 1 Activities: The Physics of AI

## Activity 1: Ice Breaker -- "AI or Human?"

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Group Size:** Any
**Materials:** Projector/screen, prepared slides with 10 text samples

### Facilitation Guide

**Setup (2 minutes):**
Display a series of short text passages on the screen. Some were written by a human, some were generated by AI. Participants vote on each one.

**How to Play:**

1. Show each passage one at a time (30--45 seconds per passage).
2. Participants hold up a card, raise a hand, or use a digital poll:
   - Left hand / Green card = **Human**
   - Right hand / Red card = **AI**
3. After votes are in, reveal the answer and briefly discuss why.

**The 10 Passages:**

| # | Passage | Answer |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | "The quarterly earnings exceeded expectations by 12%, driven primarily by strong performance in the Asia-Pacific region." | Human |
| 2 | "The sunset painted the sky in hues of orange and pink, casting a warm glow over the tranquil lake below, where ducks glided gracefully across the mirrored surface." | AI |
| 3 | "Look, I'm not going to sugarcoat it -- the project's behind schedule and we need all hands on deck this weekend." | Human |
| 4 | "Machine learning algorithms can be broadly categorized into three main types: supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning. Each approach has its own strengths and weaknesses." | AI |
| 5 | "I tried the new coffee place on 5th. Honestly? Mid. The espresso was bitter and they gave me attitude when I asked for oat milk." | Human |
| 6 | "In conclusion, it is imperative that organizations adopt a comprehensive and multifaceted approach to digital transformation, ensuring that all stakeholders are aligned and engaged throughout the process." | AI |
| 7 | "The test results came back and there's a weird anomaly in batch 47 -- the tensile strength dropped 15% but I can't figure out why. Sending samples to the lab again." | Human |
| 8 | "Effective communication in the workplace involves active listening, clear articulation of ideas, empathy towards colleagues, and a willingness to provide and receive constructive feedback." | AI |
| 9 | "tbh the new policy makes no sense for our team. We're already doing half of this stuff manually and now they want us to document it differently??" | Human |
| 10 | "The integration of artificial intelligence into manufacturing processes represents a paradigm shift that promises to enhance efficiency, reduce costs, and improve overall product quality across the value chain." | AI |

**Scoring:**
- 8--10 correct: "AI Whisperer"
- 5--7 correct: "Getting There"
- 0--4 correct: "Don't Worry -- That's Why You're Here!"

**Facilitator Tips:**
- Emphasize that even experts struggle with this -- it highlights why understanding AI fundamentals matters.
- Use this as a natural segue: "The fact that this is hard tells us something important about modern AI..."
- If the group is quiet, ask volunteers to explain their reasoning before revealing answers.

**Debrief Questions (2 minutes):**
1. What clues did you look for when guessing?
2. Were you surprised by any of the answers?
3. What does this tell us about the current state of AI?

## Activity 2: Mid-Session Energizer -- "Token Auction"

**Duration:** 12 minutes
**Group Size:** Teams of 3--5
**Materials:** Play money (or point trackers), auction item list, timer

### Full Auction Rules

**Setup:**
Each team starts with **1,000 tokens** (the currency). The facilitator acts as auctioneer. Teams bid on items that represent AI concepts. The goal is to build the most valuable "AI toolkit" by the end.

**Auction Process:**
1. The facilitator reads the item description aloud.
2. Bidding starts at the listed minimum bid.
3. Teams raise paddles (or hands) to bid in increments of 50 tokens.
4. Highest bidder wins the item.
5. After all items are sold, points are tallied.

**Auction Item List:**

| Item | Description | Min Bid | Point Value | Why It's Valuable |
|------|-------------|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Mega Context Window | Process 1 million tokens at once | 100 | 150 | Lets you work with massive documents |
| Temperature Dial | Fine-tune creativity vs. precision | 50 | 100 | Essential for controlling output quality |
| Hallucination Detector | Flags when AI makes things up | 100 | 200 | Critical for trust and accuracy |
| RAG Module | Connect AI to your company's real data | 150 | 250 | Grounds AI in factual, current info |
| Shadow AI Shield | Detects unauthorized AI use in your org | 100 | 175 | Protects company data and compliance |
| Prompt Library | 500 pre-tested prompts for business | 50 | 75 | Saves time, not game-changing |
| Multi-Modal Engine | Process text, images, audio, and video | 100 | 125 | Versatile but expensive |
| Fine-Tuning Kit | Customize a model for your industry | 150 | 200 | Powerful for specialized tasks |
| Real-Time Fact Checker | Cross-references outputs with live data | 100 | 175 | Reduces errors in real-time |
| AI Ethics Compass | Guides responsible AI use decisions | 50 | 150 | Often undervalued but essential |

**Scoring:**
- Add up the point values of all items won.
- Subtract any unspent tokens (hoarding is penalized).
- Bonus: +100 points if a team acquired both "Hallucination Detector" AND "RAG Module" (they work together).
- Bonus: +75 points if a team acquired both "Shadow AI Shield" AND "AI Ethics Compass" (governance combo).

**Facilitator Tips:**
- Ham it up as an auctioneer -- energy is everything.
- Read the "Why It's Valuable" column after each sale to reinforce learning.
- If a team is hoarding tokens, remind them unspent tokens are penalized.
- Use dramatic pauses: "Going once... going twice..."

**Debrief Questions:**
1. Which item did you want most? Why?
2. Did any item sell for more or less than you expected?
3. How does this map to real decisions organizations make about AI investments?

## Activity 3: Understanding Check -- "Concept Map Builder"

**Duration:** 15 minutes
**Group Size:** Pairs or trios
**Materials:** Printed template (below), pens/markers, or digital whiteboard

### Concept Map Template

**Nodes (pre-printed on the template):**

```
                        [TOKEN]
                       /       \
                      /         \
                     ?           ?
                    /             \
        [CONTEXT WINDOW]     [TEMPERATURE]
                |                  |
                ?                  ?
                |                  |
        [HALLUCINATION]  <--?-->  [RAG]
                \                 /
                 \               /
                  ?             ?
                   \           /
                   [SHADOW AI]
```

**Instructions for Participants:**

1. Each "?" represents a connection line that needs a label.
2. Write a short phrase (3--8 words) on each line explaining the relationship.
3. You may add up to 3 additional connection lines if you see relationships not shown.

**Answer Key (for facilitator):**

| Connection | Suggested Label |
|------------|----------------|
| Token --> Context Window | "Tokens fill up the context window" |
| Token --> Temperature | "Temperature affects how tokens are selected" |
| Context Window --> Hallucination | "Limited context can cause hallucinations" |
| Temperature --> RAG | "Lower temperature + RAG = more accurate" |
| Hallucination <--> RAG | "RAG reduces hallucinations with real data" |
| Hallucination --> Shadow AI | "Unmanaged AI increases hallucination risk" |
| RAG --> Shadow AI | "RAG systems need governance to prevent shadow AI" |

**Bonus Connections Participants Might Add:**
- Context Window --> RAG: "RAG retrieves content that uses context window space"
- Temperature --> Hallucination: "High temperature increases hallucination risk"
- Shadow AI --> Token: "Shadow AI burns tokens without oversight"

**Scoring Rubric:**
- Each correct/reasonable connection label: 2 points
- Each valid bonus connection: 3 points
- Creativity bonus for insightful labels: 1 point each
- Maximum score: 25 points

**Facilitator Tips:**
- Walk around and check for misconceptions early.
- If pairs are stuck, give one hint: "Think about what happens when the context window fills up."
- Have 2--3 pairs share their maps and compare approaches.

**Debrief Questions:**
1. Which connection was hardest to label? Why?
2. Did any pair find a connection that surprised others?
3. How do these concepts interact in your daily work?

## Activity 4: Quick Quiz Game -- "Lightning Round"

**Duration:** 8 minutes
**Group Size:** Individual or teams
**Materials:** Projector, timer (8 seconds per question)

### 10 Rapid-Fire Questions

Display each question for 8 seconds. Participants write answers on paper or use a polling tool.

**Question 1:** What is the basic unit of text that an AI model processes?
**Answer:** A token

**Question 2:** If a model has a 128K context window, what does that roughly mean?
**Answer:** It can process approximately 128,000 tokens (about 96,000 words) in a single conversation

**Question 3:** What happens when you set the temperature to 0?
**Answer:** The model gives the most deterministic/predictable response (least random)

**Question 4:** What is the term for when an AI confidently generates false information?
**Answer:** Hallucination

**Question 5:** RAG stands for what?
**Answer:** Retrieval-Augmented Generation

**Question 6:** True or False: A larger context window always means better answers.
**Answer:** False -- larger context windows can introduce noise and irrelevant information

**Question 7:** What is "Shadow AI"?
**Answer:** Unauthorized or unmanaged use of AI tools within an organization

**Question 8:** Name one way to reduce AI hallucinations.
**Answer:** Any of: Use RAG, lower temperature, provide clear context, ask for citations, use grounding data

**Question 9:** What is the main risk of Shadow AI for a company?
**Answer:** Data leakage / security risks / compliance violations / loss of governance

**Question 10:** If you want a creative brainstorming response, should temperature be high or low?
**Answer:** High (closer to 1.0)

**Scoring:**
- 9--10 correct: "Unit 1 Master"
- 7--8 correct: "Solid Foundation"
- 5--6 correct: "Review Recommended"
- Below 5: "Let's Revisit the Key Concepts Together"

**Facilitator Tips:**
- Keep energy high -- use a countdown timer on screen.
- For wrong answers, give a 15-second explanation rather than moving on silently.
- Consider doing a "phone a friend" option where someone can ask a neighbor for one question.

## Activity 5: Group Discussion Prompts -- "AI in Our Workplace"

**Duration:** 15 minutes (3 minutes per question)
**Group Size:** Tables of 4--6
**Materials:** Discussion prompt cards (one per table), flip chart paper, markers

### The 5 Discussion Questions

**Prompt 1: The Shadow AI Question**
"Think about your own team. What tools or AI applications might people be using without IT's knowledge? What risks does this create, and how would you address it without killing innovation?"

*Facilitator guidance: Push groups to be honest. Most teams have some shadow AI. The goal is not to shame but to surface reality.*

**Prompt 2: The Context Window Dilemma**
"Your team needs to analyze a 200-page contract using an AI tool with a 32K token context window. The whole document won't fit. What's your strategy? What might go wrong?"

*Facilitator guidance: Look for answers involving chunking, summarization, prioritizing sections, or using RAG. Flag the risk of missing cross-references between sections.*

**Prompt 3: The Temperature Trade-Off**
"You're using AI to draft a safety report for a manufacturing process AND to brainstorm marketing taglines for a new product. Should you use the same AI settings for both? Why or why not?"

*Facilitator guidance: This should surface the temperature concept. Safety report = low temperature (precision). Marketing = high temperature (creativity). Discuss consequences of getting this wrong.*

**Prompt 4: The Hallucination Incident**
"An employee used AI to generate a technical specification and sent it to a client without checking. The AI hallucinated a material property that doesn't exist. The client is now asking questions. What do you do? How do you prevent this in the future?"

*Facilitator guidance: Focus on both the immediate response (transparency, correction) and the systemic fix (verification workflows, human-in-the-loop policies).*

**Prompt 5: The ROI Question**
"Your CEO asks: 'We're spending money on AI tools. Show me the return.' Using what you've learned about tokens, context windows, and RAG, how would you frame the value of AI in terms your CEO would understand?"

*Facilitator guidance: Encourage participants to think in terms of time saved, error reduction, consistency, and competitive advantage rather than just technology features.*

### Reporting Format
Each table picks a spokesperson to share their top insight in 60 seconds. Facilitator captures key themes on a whiteboard.

**Facilitator Tips:**
- Set a timer for each question -- discussions can run long.
- If a table goes quiet, sit with them briefly and ask a follow-up.
- Connect responses back to Unit 1 concepts explicitly: "That's a great example of why understanding context windows matters."
- Save the whiteboard notes -- they can be referenced in later units.

**Debrief Questions:**
1. Which question sparked the most debate at your table?
2. Did any discussion change your perspective on AI in your role?
3. What's one thing you'll do differently starting tomorrow?
