## Activity 1: Ice Breaker -- "Microsoft Bingo"

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Group Size:** Any
**Materials:** Printed bingo cards (below), pens/daubers, small prizes (optional)

### Full 5x5 Bingo Card

Participants mingle and find colleagues who match each square. Each person can only sign one square per card. First to complete a line (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal) calls "BINGO!"

| | B | I | N | G | O |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Has accidentally replied-all on an email | Uses keyboard shortcuts in Excel | Has more than 10 unread Teams channels | Can name 3 Microsoft 365 apps | Has used Copilot before today |
| **2** | Has created a PowerPoint in the last week | Has attended a Teams meeting with 20+ people | Knows what OneDrive is for | Has used Word's Track Changes feature | Has pinned a Teams chat |
| **3** | Has shared a file via SharePoint | Has used Excel formulas beyond SUM | **FREE SPACE: I'm here to learn about AI!** | Has customized their Teams status | Has searched in Outlook using filters |
| **4** | Has created a Teams channel | Knows the difference between Teams Chat and Teams Channel | Has used Microsoft Forms | Has co-authored a document in real time | Has recorded a Teams meeting |
| **5** | Has used Focus mode in any M365 app | Can explain what SharePoint does | Has used conditional formatting in Excel | Has scheduled a Teams meeting with a poll | Has uploaded a file to Teams |

### Rules

1. When the facilitator says "GO," participants stand and mingle.
2. Approach someone and ask if they match a square on your card.
3. If yes, they sign that square. Each person can only sign YOUR card once.
4. First person to complete a line shouts "BINGO!" and reads their line aloud.
5. Continue playing for a "blackout" (all squares filled) if time allows.

**Facilitator Tips:**
- Play upbeat music during the mingle.
- If the group is small (under 12), allow each person to sign 2 squares per card.
- Use this to gauge the group's M365 familiarity -- listen for which squares are hardest to fill.
- The "Has used Copilot before today" square is deliberately diagnostic.

**Debrief Questions:**
1. Which squares were hardest to find someone for?
2. How many of you had already used Copilot? (Show of hands -- this tells you your baseline.)
3. Which M365 features in the bingo card were you NOT familiar with?

## Activity 2: Energizer -- "Copilot Charades"

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Group Size:** Teams of 4--6
**Materials:** Feature cards (below), timer (60 seconds per round)

### 10 Features to Act Out

One team member draws a card and acts out the Copilot feature WITHOUT speaking. Their team guesses. 60 seconds per turn, teams alternate.

| Card # | Feature to Act Out | Hints for Acting |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| 1 | **Copilot summarizing a long email thread** | Mime reading a very long document, then shrinking it down, then looking relieved |
| 2 | **Copilot generating a PowerPoint from a Word document** | Mime typing a document, then swiping/transforming it, then presenting slides |
| 3 | **Copilot creating an Excel formula** | Look confused at numbers, ask for help (pointing up), then celebrate as numbers appear |
| 4 | **Copilot drafting a reply to an email** | Read something (look annoyed), snap fingers, then review and nod approvingly |
| 5 | **Copilot transcribing a Teams meeting** | Mime talking in a meeting, point to words appearing, scroll through transcript |
| 6 | **Copilot catching you up on missed Teams messages** | Mime sleeping/being away, return, look overwhelmed, then calm as a summary appears |
| 7 | **Copilot analyzing data in Excel** | Point at spreadsheet, shrug in confusion, ask Copilot, then mime reading insights with nodding |
| 8 | **Copilot creating a meeting agenda** | Check calendar, look stressed, type a question, then organize items on a list |
| 9 | **Copilot rewriting text in a different tone** | Read something and look unhappy with it, adjust an imaginary dial, read again and look satisfied |
| 10 | **Copilot finding a file across M365** | Search frantically through folders, type a question, then pull up the exact file triumphantly |

### Scoring
- Correct guess within 30 seconds: 3 points
- Correct guess within 60 seconds: 1 point
- No correct guess: 0 points

**Facilitator Tips:**
- Encourage big, dramatic gestures -- this is supposed to be fun and energizing.
- If someone is shy about performing, let them partner with a teammate.
- After each correct guess, spend 15 seconds confirming what Copilot actually does for that feature.
- This is physical activity designed to break up sedentary learning -- get people standing.

**Debrief Questions:**
1. Which feature surprised you the most? (You didn't know Copilot could do that?)
2. Which feature would save you the most time in your daily work?
3. Did acting it out help you remember what the feature does?

**Duration:** 20 minutes
**Group Size:** Pairs
**Materials:** Scavenger hunt list (below), M365 access with Copilot enabled, scoring sheet

Pairs work through the list, finding and demonstrating each feature. They earn points for each completed item and take a screenshot as proof.

| 1 | Open Copilot in Microsoft Word | Ribbon bar or right-side panel | 5 | Easy |
| 2 | Ask Copilot to summarize a document in Word | Copilot panel > "Summarize this document" | 5 | Easy |
| 3 | Use Copilot to draft new content in Word | Click "Draft with Copilot" or type "/" in document | 10 | Easy |

| 5 | Open Copilot in Excel | Ribbon bar or right-side panel in a table-formatted sheet | 5 | Easy |

| 7 | Ask Copilot to analyze data trends in Excel | Copilot panel > "Show data insights" or ask about trends | 15 | Medium |
| 8 | Use Copilot to create a PowerPoint presentation | Open PowerPoint > Copilot > "Create a presentation about..." | 10 | Easy |
| 9 | Use Copilot to add a slide from a prompt | In an existing deck > Copilot > "Add a slide about..." | 10 | Medium |
| 10 | Open Copilot in Outlook | New email or reply > Copilot button | 5 | Easy |
| 11 | Ask Copilot to draft an email in Outlook | Copilot > "Draft an email to..." | 10 | Easy |
| 12 | Ask Copilot to summarize an email thread in Outlook | Open a thread > Copilot > "Summarize" | 10 | Medium |
| 13 | Use Copilot in a Teams meeting (or view meeting recap) | During/after meeting > Copilot > recap/summary | 15 | Hard |

**Total Possible Points: 155**

### Scoring Tiers

| Points | Badge |
|--------|-------|
| 130--155 | "Copilot Power User" |
| 100--129 | "Copilot Explorer" |
| 60--99 | "Copilot Curious" |
| Below 60 | "Copilot Newbie -- and That's OK!" |

### Bonus Challenges (+10 points each)

- **Bonus 1:** Find a Copilot feature not on this list and demo it.
- **Bonus 2:** Use Copilot across 2 different M365 apps in a single workflow (e.g., summarize email in Outlook, then create a PowerPoint from the summary).
- **Bonus 3:** Ask Copilot to do something it CANNOT do and document its response.

**Facilitator Tips:**
- Ensure M365 with Copilot licenses is set up and working BEFORE the session. Test every feature.
- Some features require data to already exist (e.g., email threads, Excel tables). Pre-load sample data if needed.
- Walk around and help pairs who get stuck -- the goal is discovery, not frustration.
- If Copilot access is limited, demo some features on the projector and have pairs take notes.

**Debrief Questions:**
1. Which feature was easiest to find and use?
2. Which feature do you think will be most valuable for your role?
3. Did Copilot surprise you -- either positively or negatively -- during the hunt?
4. What did you discover Copilot CANNOT do?

## Activity 4: M365 Feature Discovery Challenge

**Duration:** 25 minutes
**Group Size:** Teams of 3--4 (one team per app)
**Materials:** M365 access, flip chart paper, markers, timer

### Setup

Each team is assigned ONE M365 application with Copilot. They become the "experts" on that app's Copilot features and then teach the rest of the group.

**Team Assignments:**

| Team | Application | Focus Areas |
|------|------------|-------------|
| Team 1 | Word + Copilot | Drafting, rewriting, summarizing, formatting |
| Team 2 | Excel + Copilot | Formulas, analysis, charts, data insights |
| Team 3 | PowerPoint + Copilot | Presentation creation, slide design, speaker notes |
| Team 4 | Outlook + Copilot | Email drafting, thread summaries, scheduling |
| Team 5 | Teams + Copilot | Meeting recaps, chat summaries, channel catch-up |
| Team 6 | Copilot Chat | Cross-app queries, file search, general assistance |

### Phase 1: Exploration (12 minutes)
Each team explores their assigned app's Copilot features. They must document:
- 3 things Copilot does well in this app
- 1 limitation or gotcha they discovered
- 1 "wow moment" -- something that genuinely impressed them
- Their top tip for other users

### Phase 2: Presentation (2 minutes per team = 12 minutes)
Each team presents their findings to the group. Format:
1. Quick demo (show one feature live)
2. Share their top 3 capabilities
3. Warn about the limitation
4. Share the "wow moment"
5. Give their top tip

### Presentation Template (flip chart)

```
APP NAME: _______________

Top 3 Copilot Features:
1. ______________________
2. ______________________
3. ______________________

Watch Out For:
_________________________

Wow Moment:
_________________________

Our #1 Tip:
_________________________
```

**Facilitator Tips:**
- Assign teams to apps strategically -- put Excel-heavy teams on Excel, etc. Or deliberately mismatch for cross-training.
- During exploration, circulate and drop hints: "Have you tried asking Copilot to..."
- Keep presentations to 2 minutes strict -- use a visible timer.
- After all presentations, do a "feature comparison" discussion: "Which app's Copilot seemed most useful to you personally?"

**Debrief Questions:**
1. Which team's presentation made you most excited to try their app?
2. Did any limitations surprise you?
3. How would you combine features across apps in your daily workflow?

## Activity 5: "Before AI vs After AI" Workflow Comparison

**Duration:** 20 minutes
**Group Size:** Pairs or trios
**Materials:** Workflow template (below), sticky notes or digital whiteboard, timer

### Exercise Instructions

Participants map a real workflow they do regularly -- first as they do it today (Before AI), then as they could do it with Copilot (After AI).

### Workflow Template

**Step 1: Choose Your Workflow**

**Step 5: Present to Group (7 minutes total)**
Each pair gets 90 seconds to share:
- The workflow they chose
- The biggest time savings
- The most impactful Copilot feature for that workflow
- Any step that CANNOT be replaced by AI (and why)

### Example: "Preparing for a Weekly Team Meeting"

**Before AI:**
1. Check calendar for attendees (Outlook, 2 min)
2. Review last week's meeting notes (OneNote, 5 min)
3. Scroll through Teams messages for updates (Teams, 15 min)
4. Email team asking for agenda items (Outlook, 5 min)
5. Wait for responses (varies, 1--24 hours)
6. Compile agenda in Word (Word, 10 min)
7. Create any needed slides (PowerPoint, 20 min)
8. Send agenda to team (Outlook, 3 min)
**Total: ~60 min + waiting time**

**After AI:**
1. Ask Copilot to summarize last meeting + Teams updates (Copilot Chat, 2 min)
2. Ask Copilot to draft agenda based on summary + open action items (Word + Copilot, 3 min)
3. Review and edit agenda -- add human judgment (Word, 5 min)
4. Ask Copilot to create slides from agenda (PowerPoint + Copilot, 3 min)
5. Send agenda via Outlook with Copilot-drafted cover note (Outlook, 2 min)
**Total: ~15 min, no waiting**

**Facilitator Tips:**
- Stress that "After AI" should still include human review steps -- AI assists, humans decide.
- If participants struggle to identify a workflow, help them think about what they did yesterday.
- The most powerful learning happens when participants see their OWN time savings, not hypothetical ones.
- Take photos of the best before/after comparisons to share in a follow-up email.

**Debrief Questions:**
1. What was the average time savings across the room?
2. Which steps were participants most relieved to hand off to AI?
3. Which steps did everyone agree should stay human?
4. How would you measure the quality of AI-assisted outputs vs. your manual work?
