# Ice Breaker Activities - APC AI Training Program

> One ice breaker per unit, designed to energize participants and introduce each unit's theme.

---

## Unit 1: "AI or Human?"

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Theme:** Introduction to AI & Generative AI

### Instructions

1. **Setup (2 min):** Display 8 outputs on the projector -- a mix of text paragraphs, short poems, and email drafts. Half were written by a human, half by an AI (e.g., Gemini, ChatGPT).
2. **Activity (5 min):** Each participant receives a voting card (or uses a digital poll). For each output, they vote: **AI** or **Human**.
3. **Reveal & Discuss (3 min):** Reveal the correct answers one by one. Ask the top scorer to share their reasoning.

### Materials

- 8 pre-prepared text samples (4 human, 4 AI-generated) -- mix Arabic and English
- Voting cards (green = Human, red = AI) or a live polling tool (e.g., Mentimeter)
- Projector / shared screen
- Scoreboard (whiteboard or slide)

### Facilitation Tips

- Choose outputs that are genuinely difficult to distinguish -- avoid obvious tells.
- Include at least 2 samples relevant to APC (e.g., a safety briefing, a production report summary).
- If participants are shy, let them discuss in pairs before voting.
- Emphasize that being "fooled" is the point -- AI is getting remarkably capable.

### Debrief Questions

1. What clues did you look for to distinguish AI from human writing?
2. Were you surprised by any of the reveals? Which ones and why?
3. What does this tell us about the current state of AI capabilities?
4. How might this ability to generate human-like text affect our work at APC?

## Unit 2: "Prompt Roulette"

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Theme:** Prompt Engineering Fundamentals

### Instructions

1. **Setup (2 min):** Prepare three jars/bags containing word cards:
   - **Jar A -- Roles:** Marketing Manager, Safety Officer, CFO, Chemist, HR Director, Plant Engineer
   - **Jar B -- Tasks:** Write a report, Create a checklist, Draft an email, Summarize data, Generate ideas, Explain a process
   - **Jar C -- Constraints:** In 50 words, Using bullet points, For a non-technical audience, In Arabic, With a table, As a poem
2. **Activity (6 min):** Each participant (or pair) draws one card from each jar. They have 3 minutes to construct the best possible prompt combining all three elements. Write it on a sticky note or shared doc.
3. **Showcase (2 min):** 3-4 volunteers read their prompts aloud. The group votes on the most creative and the most practical.

### Materials

- Three jars/bags with labeled word cards (min 6 cards each)
- Sticky notes and markers (or shared Google Doc / Jamboard)
- Timer visible to all participants

### Facilitation Tips

- Encourage participants to think about the CO-STAR framework even before it is formally taught -- this plants a seed.
- If a combination seems impossible, that is part of the fun -- creativity under constraints.
- Have a few backup combinations ready in case someone draws a truly awkward set.

### Debrief Questions

1. What made some prompts better than others?
2. Did the constraint help or hinder your creativity?
3. What would you add to your prompt to make the AI response even better?
4. How does specifying a role change the kind of response you might get?

## Unit 3: "Microsoft Bingo"

**Duration:** 8 minutes
**Theme:** Microsoft Copilot in M365

### Instructions

1. **Setup (1 min):** Distribute pre-printed Bingo cards. Each square contains a Microsoft 365 + Copilot feature (e.g., "Copilot in Teams can summarize meetings," "Excel Copilot can generate formulas," "Edge sidebar can summarize PDFs").
2. **Activity (5 min):** The facilitator reads out scenario descriptions one at a time (e.g., "You need to quickly catch up on a meeting you missed"). Participants mark the square that matches the feature that would solve that scenario.
3. **Winner (2 min):** First person to get a line (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal) calls "Bingo!" and explains each marked square.

### Materials

- Pre-printed 5x5 Bingo cards (at least 4 variations to prevent simultaneous wins)
- List of 25 scenario descriptions mapped to features
- Small prizes (optional -- APC-branded pens, stickers)
- Markers or stamps for marking squares

### Bingo Card Features (25 total -- sample)

| Copilot in Word: Draft document | Copilot in Excel: Analyze data | Copilot in Teams: Summarize meeting | Copilot in Outlook: Draft reply | Copilot in PowerPoint: Create slides |
| Edge Sidebar: Summarize page | Copilot Chat: Ask questions | Word: Rewrite text | Excel: Generate formula | Teams: Generate action items |
| Outlook: Prioritize inbox | PowerPoint: Add speaker notes | FREE SPACE | Edge: Compare pages | Copilot: Generate images |
| Word: Format document | Excel: Create charts | Teams: Translate message | Outlook: Schedule meeting | PowerPoint: Design slides |
| Edge: Translate page | Copilot Chat: Write code | Word: Summarize document | Excel: Highlight patterns | Teams: Create agenda |

### Facilitation Tips

- Read scenarios at a steady pace -- not too fast for non-native English speakers.
- Have scenarios in both English and Arabic available.
- If no one gets Bingo quickly, switch to "four corners" or "any 5 squares."

### Debrief Questions

1. Which features were you already aware of?
2. Which features surprised you the most?
3. Which feature do you think would be most useful in your daily work?
4. Are there any squares you are most excited to try out today?

## Unit 4: "Department Swap"

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Theme:** APC-Specific Prompt Engineering

### Instructions

1. **Setup (2 min):** Participants form groups of 3-4, ideally mixing departments. Each group receives a card describing a challenge from a **different** department than any member belongs to.
2. **Activity (6 min):** The group must:
   - Read the challenge (e.g., "HR needs to screen 200 CVs for a maintenance technician role")
   - Discuss how they would approach it, even though it is not their expertise
   - Write one AI prompt that could help solve this challenge
3. **Share (2 min):** Each group reads their prompt. The "real" department members in the room rate it: Would this actually help?

### Department Challenge Cards

- **HR:** Screen 200 CVs for a maintenance technician position, shortlisting top 10 candidates.
- **Production:** Analyze last quarter's potash output data to identify the top 3 efficiency bottlenecks.
- **Maintenance:** Create a predictive maintenance schedule for 15 conveyor belt systems.
- **Finance:** Summarize the monthly budget variance report for executive presentation.
- **Safety:** Draft a toolbox talk on chemical handling for new employees.
- **IT:** Write a user guide for the new ERP module rollout.
- **Procurement:** Compare 4 supplier quotes for industrial-grade sodium chloride.
- **Quality:** Create an SOP for potash purity testing procedures.

### Materials

- Department challenge cards (printed, one per group)
- Sticky notes or paper for writing prompts
- Timer

### Facilitation Tips

- Intentionally assign groups challenges from departments none of them work in -- this is the fun part.
- Encourage them to ask: "What would I need to know to do this well?" -- that thinking translates directly to good prompting.
- Keep the mood light; the goal is empathy and cross-functional thinking, not perfection.

### Debrief Questions

1. What was hardest about writing a prompt for a department you do not work in?
2. What information did you wish you had?
3. How does this exercise relate to giving AI enough context in your prompts?
4. Did any group's prompt surprise the "real" department members?

## Unit 5: "Spot the AI Art"

**Duration:** 8 minutes
**Theme:** AI Image Generation & Multimodal AI

### Instructions

1. **Setup (1 min):** Display 10 images on the projector in a numbered grid. Half are real photographs, half are AI-generated images (from Gemini, DALL-E, Midjourney, etc.). Choose a mix of landscapes, people, industrial scenes, and objects.
2. **Activity (5 min):** Participants write down their guesses (AI or Real) for each numbered image. They should also note *why* they think so for at least 3 images.
3. **Reveal & Discuss (2 min):** Reveal answers. Highlight the telltale signs of AI art (hands, text, reflections, symmetry artifacts).

### Materials

- 10 images (5 real, 5 AI-generated) displayed on projector -- include at least 2 industrial/mining-related images
- Numbered answer sheets
- Slide with "AI Art Tells" summary (hands, text, reflections, impossible physics, over-smoothing)

### Facilitation Tips

- Choose high-quality AI images that are genuinely difficult to spot -- this is more engaging than obvious fakes.
- Include one APC-related image (e.g., Dead Sea landscape, potash processing plant) to make it relevant.
- If time permits, let the top scorer explain their detection strategy.

### Debrief Questions

1. Which images were hardest to classify? Why?
2. What are the most reliable "tells" for AI-generated images?
3. How could AI-generated images be useful at APC? Where could they be risky?
4. How do you think AI image generation will evolve in the next 2-3 years?

## Unit 6: "Research Race"

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Theme:** NotebookLM & Research Tools

### Instructions

1. **Setup (2 min):** Divide into teams of 2-3. Present a research question relevant to APC (see below). Each team is assigned a different tool: Google Search, Gemini Chat, NotebookLM (with pre-loaded sources), Wikipedia.
2. **Activity (6 min):** Each team must find the answer using ONLY their assigned tool. They must also note:
   - How many steps it took
   - How confident they are in the answer (1-5)
   - Whether the answer includes a source citation
3. **Compare (2 min):** Teams share their answers, step counts, confidence, and citation status. Compare across tools.

### Research Questions (choose 1 per round)

- "What is the global market share of potash by country for the most recent year available?"
- "What are the three most common methods of potash extraction, and how do they compare in energy efficiency?"
- "What environmental regulations apply to Dead Sea mineral extraction in Jordan?"
- "What is the chemical process for converting carnallite to potassium chloride?"

### Materials

- Laptops/tablets for each team (with tools pre-loaded)
- NotebookLM workspace pre-loaded with 3-4 relevant PDFs/articles
- Timer
- Comparison matrix on whiteboard (Tool | Answer | Steps | Confidence | Citation)

### Facilitation Tips

- Pre-load NotebookLM with genuinely useful sources so that team has a fair chance.
- The point is not to crown a winner but to show that different tools excel in different contexts.
- If internet is unreliable, have backup screenshots of search results ready.

### Debrief Questions

1. Which tool gave the fastest answer? The most accurate? The best-cited?
2. When would you choose each tool for your real work?
3. How does having pre-loaded sources (NotebookLM) change the research process?
4. What surprised you about the comparison?

## Unit 7: "Data Detective"

**Duration:** 8 minutes
**Theme:** Data Analysis with AI

### Instructions

1. **Setup (1 min):** Display a chart or dashboard on screen that contains one deliberate anomaly or error. The chart should look professional and relate to APC operations.
2. **Activity (5 min):** Participants study the chart individually for 2 minutes, then discuss in pairs for 3 minutes. Each pair writes down:
   - The anomaly they found
   - What might have caused it
   - What question they would ask an AI tool to investigate further
3. **Reveal (2 min):** Reveal the anomaly. Discuss the AI prompt that would best help investigate it.

### Sample Charts (prepare 2-3 options)

- **Production Chart:** Monthly potash output with one month showing 40% above capacity (impossible spike).
- **Safety Dashboard:** Incident rate chart where Q3 shows zero incidents across all departments (suspiciously perfect).
- **Financial Chart:** Cost per ton trending down while energy costs trend up in the same period (contradictory trends).

### Materials

- Projected chart/dashboard (PowerPoint or printed handouts)
- Paper and pens for notes
- "Reveal" slide with the anomaly highlighted and explained

### Facilitation Tips

- Make the anomaly findable but not immediately obvious -- participants should need to think.
- If some participants spot it instantly, ask them to also find a *second* potential issue (there often is one).
- Connect this directly to the unit theme: AI can help spot anomalies, but you need to know what to look for.

### Debrief Questions

1. How long did it take you to spot the anomaly?
2. What made you suspicious?
3. How would you prompt an AI to find this kind of anomaly in your real data?
4. What are the risks of trusting AI to find anomalies without human oversight?

## Unit 8: "Pitch Your AI"

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Theme:** Capstone & Future Vision

### Instructions

1. **Setup (2 min):** Each participant gets a blank "Elevator Pitch" card with the template:
   - **Problem:** (one sentence)
   - **AI Solution:** (one sentence)
   - **Tools:** (list the AI tools from this program you would use)
   - **Impact:** (one sentence on expected benefit)
2. **Activity (5 min):** Participants fill out their card with a real AI application idea for their department at APC. This can be a preview of their capstone project or a new idea.
3. **Speed Pitches (3 min):** 4-5 volunteers deliver their pitch in 30 seconds each. The group applauds the most compelling.

### Materials

- Printed "Elevator Pitch" cards (or half-sheets of paper with the template)
- Markers
- Timer (30-second intervals for pitches)
- Optional: small trophy or certificate for "Best Pitch"

### Facilitation Tips

- Reassure participants that this is low-stakes and informal -- no wrong answers.
- If someone is stuck, suggest they think about their most repetitive or frustrating daily task.
- This activity doubles as a capstone project brainstorming session -- encourage them to keep their cards.
- Celebrate every pitch to build confidence for the actual capstone presentations.

### Debrief Questions

1. What common themes did you notice across the pitches?
2. Which pitch idea do you think would have the biggest impact at APC?
3. How did this program change the way you think about AI at work?
4. What is one thing you will do differently starting next week?
